Manifest of the 97 files · External originals and download links · External register as CSV

Each ZIP contains a CONTENTS.csv. The manifest names the underlying issuer separately from the source of the deposited copy. It records the edition, extent, local format, limitations, URL and SHA-256. Some holdings are mirror PDFs; others are transcriptions, extracts or filtered data, not the publishing institution’s original file. The external register includes originals, alternative editions, catalogues and status leads; none is counted as a local file merely because it has a link. Several publishers’ binaries could not be transferred to this workspace. A digest proves which bytes were supplied, not assent, legal currency, court certification or permission to republish.

Constitution: where to check the text

Page quality. The page check covers both possible readings of “pp. 15–43 and 129–130”: PDF pages and printed folios. Their union in this edition is physical PDF pp. 15–49, 129–130 and 135–136 (39 pages). The rendered pages showed no visible clipping there. The smallest text-box clearances across them were left 23.68, top 54.03, right 22.97 and bottom 6.83 points. This is a check on this copy and these ranges, not a whole-document certification. The previously described docs/constitution.pdf was not in the checkout; it was not overwritten.

Official-edition leads. The MoJ edition contains Attorney-General Adoke’s direction to the Federal Government Printer, but extends only through the Third Alteration. NILDS’s 2023 repository entry is a lead for the edition through Fifth, not proof of a certified through-2026 consolidation. Obtain the title page, imprint, alteration cut-off and any later authenticated Acts before claiming the requested Grade A status.

Four provisions worth checking on the page:

  • Corrections: No. 15 (Act 22), s.2 deletes Exclusive Item 48 and inserts Concurrent para. 10A. Rail: No. 16 (Act 23), s.2 deletes Exclusive Item 55 and inserts Concurrent para. 20A. The latter distinguishes intrastate tracks/carriers from federal inter-state infrastructure and national regulation. See PLAC PDF p.232 for the deletions.
  • Electricity: No. 17 (Act 24), Gazette 75, vol. 110, 25 April 2023, Notice 44. The change is a deletion in Second Schedule, Part II, para. 14(b), not the transfer of the entire grid to states. On PLAC PDF p.238 (printed p.232), the deleted national-grid exclusion is visibly struck through. Plain-text extraction can make deleted words look operative. The scan’s internal heading also conflicts with its Fifth Alteration short title; cite the operative clause and schedule.
  • Cabinet: No. 23 (Act 25), ss.147(7) and 192(6), concerns nomination for confirmation within 60 days after the oath; it does not promise confirmation or swearing-in by day 60. See PLAC PDF pp.114 and 140.

Proposals, not enacted provisions

Local government, 2022 round. The two bill texts and the January 2023 voting table are in archive 01. For each bill the published table records 15 yes, 10 nay, 2 abstained/undecided, 9 awaiting transmission. The yes-voting states are not identical: Kano and Niger supported the financial but not the administrative bill; Imo and Kaduna did the reverse. Neither bill appeared on the list of 35 transmitted. The source itself says “Still in process”; it is a dated return table, not the signed resolutions of 36 assemblies or a final “no” from the nine outstanding states. See p.1, rows 1–2. The enacted 2023 Nos. 1 and 2 concern LGA names.

State police. Archive 01 includes all 26 clauses of the text transmitted to the states. Clauses 16–18 substitute ss.214–216; cl.19 introduces s.317A; cl.21 changes Exclusive Items 28 and 45 and adds Concurrent para. 20B; cls.22–24 alter the Third Schedule (national council, federal commission, state commissions); cl.25 alters Fifth Schedule Part II para.9. The proposed s.214 requires state legislation and national-standards certification before operational policing; it preserves federal policing in the meantime. It does not automatically convert a vigilante outfit into a police service.

The State House threshold announcement, October drafting-review notice for the National Policing Bill and 29 consequential bills, and 18 November receipt for assent are different events. The latter reported approvals in all 36 states. No presidential assent instrument, gazetted Sixth Alteration Act, enacted implementing law or signed-off operational certification was verified by the cut-off. A later date is outside this collection; absence here is not proof that no subsequent act occurred. The separate 2024 Police Act tenure amendment is a different inquiry.

Other Sixth proposals. The deposited HB.1349 is the introduced text (one additional senator per state and FCT). The later reported 157-seat design is 12 Senate + 37 House + 108 state assembly seats, not 157 national seats. Do not cite the earlier version as its final clauses. The House Order Paper identifies HB.1349/SB.440, spousal citizenship HB.1195/SB.351, independent candidacy HB.1630/SB.812 and LG third tier HBs.2121/1117 + SB.471. The 16 April town-hall document identifies s.26, the proposed LG changes to ss.2/4/5/7/162/318, court funding (Bill 29, s.162), clean environment (Bill 37, ss.33–34) and audit (Bills 43/44, ss.85/125). These are identifiable proposals; the order paper and summaries are not harmonised enacted texts, state roll calls or proof of assent.

Statutes: use the right version and jurisdiction

  • Read the whole Land Use Act, especially s.47 (PDF p.19), beside Constitution s.44 (PLAC PDF p.57 onward) and s.315(5)(d). Section 47(1) addresses vesting and grant powers; (2) expressly addresses the amount or adequacy of compensation. Do not substitute the Land Use Act’s s.44 (notices), or turn this text comparison into a holding that all land claims are barred. Constitution s.44(3) separately addresses minerals, mineral oils and natural gas. The PIA and EIA texts include schedules; the PIA transcription is noisy. Use its linked FAOLEX page images for quotations.
  • Police Act 2020: Gazette 148, vol.107, Act 2. The 2024 s.18(8A) amendment bill is not an authenticated copy of the 2024 amending Act. Federal ACJA, signed Legal Aid legislation and FREP Rules working text are local. The 2019 Correctional Service Gazette and individual state ACJLs are external. FREP forms should be checked against the issued Rules.
  • Electoral Act 2022 (Act 13) is historical. The 2026 PDF has the Clerk’s certificate on p.123 and signed assent schedule on p.124: passed 17 February, assented 18 February 2026. Its Gazette is Act 1, issue 40, vol.113, 2 March 2026. The included Lagos SIEC law is a 2017 amendment, not a nationwide or current SIEC code.
  • NDPA 2023, FOIA, original 2015 Cybercrimes Act and ONSA’s version with the 2024 amendment are local. There is a separate amending-instrument link. The Public Order Act alone is not the current law on permits: see the IGP v ANPP decision lead. A verified current NBC Code, addendum and associated decisions are not deposited.
  • Federal child-rights, disability and VAPP instruments are not proof of state enforcement. Archive 04 contains PWAN’s state-level ACJL, child-rights and VAPP tables, as reported, not certified statutes. Some ACJLs predate 2015. Check inconsistent passage/assent dates, alternative laws in Lagos and Ekiti, and the tracker’s Gombe child-rights entry against state gazettes. The FCT Customary Court Act is not a state Sharia penal code. See Harvard’s state-law sourcebook; a model code cannot be assigned to every state.
  • RMAFC Act 2025, s.19, expressly repeals Cap. R7, with savings. Both are local; neither is a bank receipt. Federal procurement, fiscal responsibility, allocation, EFCC, ICPC and Code of Conduct Bureau/Tribunal texts are present. Federal Audit Service Bill enactment was not verified; the 1956 Audit Ordinance, harmonised bill, latest PAC action and enforcement series remain to be acquired. Do not substitute proposed constitutional audit clauses for that bill.

Court records — Gate 5 remains open

First registry request: Rivers. Ask for A-G of Adamawa State & Ors v A-G of the Federation & Anor, SC/CV/329/2025, 15 December 2025: the enrolled order, Mohammed Baba Idris JSC’s lead reasons, all separate opinions and Obande Ogbuinya JSC’s dissent. Supreme Court contact: Three-Arms Zone, Abuja; info@supremecourt.gov.ng. No request or response is represented as having been sent or received.

The case alert describes a 6–1 split, a jurisdictional obstacle under s.232(1), and further observations about s.305. Reports differ in their descriptions of strike-out and dismissal. PLAC PDF p.35 reproduces s.11(4)’s proviso denying the National Assembly a power of removal under that section; pp.203–204 give the proclamation test and approvals under s.305. These are not themselves reasons of the Court. Do not publish a binding “power to suspend” holding, a quotation from the dissent or a definitive ratio/obiter classification from these leads. No CTC or authorised full report of this decision was obtained.

Local-government judgment: AGF v AG Abia State & 35 Ors, SC/CV/343/2024, 11 July 2024, (2024) LPELR-62576(SC). The local files are two paragraphs of Agim JSC on s.162 and the opening of Abiru JSC’s separate opinion agreeing only in part. They do not contain the complete funding/caretaker orders. The lead scan carries a registrar’s CTC marking dated 18 July; the Abiru scan is separate. A text excerpt from a media-hosted court document is not a CTC. For the chapter, check the actual reliefs against every opinion and the enrolled order; for implementation, check accounts, not the judgment.

SERAP education: the local text contains paras.1–32 of the merits decision, ECW/CCJ/JUD/07/10, 30 November 2010, (2010) CCJELR 199. The earlier admissibility ruling, ECW/CCJ/RUL/08/09, starts at p.183 of the official report. Paras.26–32 grant reliefs 1 and 3 as qualified, including covering the education funding shortfall; they do not order prosecution. Enforcement of the African Charter in that court is not wholesale domestic justiciability of Chapter II.

History and the “95%” claim

Archive 03 separates the promulgating Decree (recitals and ss.1–2 transcribed from a reprint, dated 5 May 1999) from a working text of the scheduled 1999 Constitution (320 sections and seven schedules, effective 29 May). The recitals report a preference to retain the 1979 Constitution with amendments and PRC approval subject to amendments. They do not identify every change, prove popular agreement, or give a percentage.

Historical originals register: the federal 1960 Constitution is Second Schedule to UK SI 1960/1652; the 1963 and 1979 texts have separate download targets. The National Library’s 1995 Vol. II is resolutions/recommendations, not Vol. I containing the draft. The 2005 NPRC catalogue, 2014 Conference report and International IDEA/Otive Igbuzor 2004 case study are also identified. These are external, not deposited historical PDFs.

For a defensible concordance, obtain the 1976 CDC draft/report and minority submission, Constituent Assembly proceedings, clean 1979 text, Niki Tobi CDCC report, PRC marked-up proofs or minutes, and the original Decree 24 Gazette. Align clauses and schedules of the unamended texts, count substantive changes separately from renumbering, disclose the denominator and preserve unmatched clauses. No original PRC markup, complete CDCC file or measured 95% result is supplied.

Reports and comparisons

The two 2026 World Bank report titles are Nigeria’s Tomorrow Must Start Today (April) and Beyond the Federal Purse: How Higher Revenues Shaped State Priorities (October). “From Higher Revenues to Better Lives” is a release headline, not the title of the October PDF. H080 contains ten indicators with edition, period, units and reported/estimated/projected status, not either full report. For national monetary poverty, the 2018/19 line of ₦137,430 per person/year is a base-period consumption threshold; record the survey, price deflator, population weights and nowcast vintage. Do not equate it with MPI or an international PPP line. See the poverty assessment.

Local: HDR 2025 statistical annex Table 1 (Nigeria HDI 0.560, rank 164; 2023 data), and the TI CPI 2025 data (Nigeria 26/100, rank 142). The CPI file uses semicolons; its field header is on line 3. Other report sources include Freedom House 2026 (20/40 political rights + 24/60 civil liberties), HRW 2026 and its 2006 non-indigene report, SBM’s July 2024–June 2025 kidnapping study, Chatham House’s March 2023 election commentary and CIVICUS. These links are not local report PDFs. A complete poverty/price microdata replication, current indigeneity case series and countrywide kidnapping incident verification are not supplied.

Archive 05 pairs Kenya’s 2010 Constitution with IPOA’s Jan–June 2024 counts (1,943 complaints; 42 referrals for prosecutorial review; four convictions recorded), and Ghana’s 1992 text with CHRAJ’s 2023 counts by mandate. None of those counts alone gives a same-cohort conviction or resolution rate. Read South Africa’s rights provisions beside the Grootboom and Treatment Action Campaign judgments; India’s Part IV/Art.37 and Art.356, Brazil’s municipal and Ministério Público provisions and Ethiopia’s federal provisions in their dated editions. The Brazil and South Africa HTML files are historical English database versions, not current official consolidations; the Amharic version controls in Ethiopia. Indonesia’s four amendments are distinct from the base text and from evidence about the military’s subsequent political role.

H095–097 contain available WDI observations for 14 country codes, including Botswana, Mauritius, Malaysia, South Korea, Singapore, Rwanda and Nigeria. Use NY.GDP.MKTP.KD.ZG for real growth, GC.REV.XGRT.GD.ZS for revenue excluding grants/GDP, and GC.XPN.TOTL.GD.ZS for expense/GDP—not all public expenditure. There are no Nigeria observations in these two fiscal extracts; periods differ elsewhere. Pre-1987 Korean growth observations are present. There is no causal test of regime type and growth, or complete comparative implementation record on county transfers, electoral commissions, Article 356 practice, prosecutors or security institutions.

Verification and outstanding gates

To verify the downloaded files, place them and manifest.csv beside verify-holdings.py and run python3 verify-holdings.py. For one ZIP: python3 verify-holdings.py --only 01-Alterations-and-proposals.zip. This reads the ZIPs without extracting them and checks the inventories, byte counts and SHA-256 digests.

To save external PDFs on a machine with access to the publishers, place get-originals.py beside originals.csv. No account or Python packages required (Python 3.8+). Without a selection it only lists records. For example:

python3 get-originals.py --id alteration-1 --id alteration-2 --id alteration-3
python3 get-originals.py --id lg-state-votes --id lg-agim --id lg-abiru
python3 get-originals.py --id ng1960 --id ng1963 --id ng1979
python3 get-originals.py --id wb-april --id wb-october
python3 get-originals.py --group faac --list
python3 get-originals.py --group faac --out downloaded-originals

New external downloads use the catalogue title and stable source ID as their filename. The script records successes and failures in receipts.jsonl, rejects obvious non-PDF/incomplete responses, and will not overwrite an existing file. Some old sites or download controls may require using the catalogue in a browser. A newly downloaded file is not automatically part of the verified manifest. Inspect imprints, editions, page completeness, signatures and permissions independently.

Private project evidence: none supplied. The NDPA is law, not project-specific safeguarding approval. There are no consent records, deidentified listening sessions (at least one urban and one rural in each of the six zones, plus two FCT sessions), interviews (at least 40 across the required languages), independent blind scores or conflicts from three reviewers, red-team replies, publishing-counsel clearance, or testing logs from 20 nonlawyer readers. No score, sample count, 80% comprehension or 75% actionability threshold is claimed. These require real participants and authorised records; keep identifiable originals and re-identification keys out of this repository. No press article, downloaded statute, template or generated text has been substituted for those materials.
